# What Are Microgreens?

Microgreens are young vegetable greens harvested just after the first true leaves have developed. These tiny plants pack an incredible nutritional punch and have become increasingly popular among health-conscious consumers and gourmet chefs alike.

## The Definition

Microgreens are harvested 7-21 days after germination, when the seedling has developed its first set of true leaves (cotyledons). This makes them larger than sprouts but smaller than baby greens, occupying a unique niche in the produce spectrum.

## Key Characteristics

- **Size**: Typically 1-3 inches tall
- **Growth stage**: Harvested after cotyledons emerge
- **Harvest time**: 7-21 days after planting
- **Edible parts**: Stem, leaves, and sometimes seed leaves
- **Flavor**: Intense, concentrated versions of the mature plant

## Microgreens vs. Sprouts vs. Baby Greens

Understanding the differences helps you choose the right option for your needs:

### Microgreens
- Grown in soil or growing medium
- Require light for photosynthesis
- Harvested with scissors above the soil line
- Lower risk of bacterial contamination
- More intense flavors

### Sprouts
- Grown in water only
- Grown in darkness
- Entire plant (including roots and seeds) is eaten
- Higher moisture content
- Mild, crunchy texture

### Baby Greens
- Older than microgreens (3-6 weeks)
- Larger leaves
- Milder flavors
- Typically used in salads

## Why Microgreens Are Special

1. **Nutritional Density**: Research shows microgreens can contain up to 40 times more nutrients than their mature counterparts
2. **Flavor Intensity**: Concentrated flavors make them perfect for garnishing and enhancing dishes
3. **Quick Growth**: Ready to harvest in 1-3 weeks
4. **Year-Round Availability**: Can be grown indoors regardless of season
5. **Sustainability**: Require minimal space and water compared to full-size vegetables

## The Science Behind Microgreens

A 2012 study by the USDA and University of Maryland found that microgreens contain significantly higher concentrations of vitamins and carotenoids than mature leaves. Red cabbage microgreens, for example, had 40 times more vitamin E and six times more vitamin C than mature red cabbage.

## Growing Microgreens

While we handle the growing for our customers, microgreens are surprisingly easy to cultivate:

1. **Seeds**: Choose high-quality, untreated seeds
2. **Medium**: Use soil, coconut coir, or hydroponic mats
3. **Light**: Provide 12-16 hours of light daily
4. **Water**: Keep medium moist but not waterlogged
5. **Harvest**: Cut above soil line when true leaves appear
